**Official syllabus (trimmed):**

> Course contents:
>  1. Introduction to Computer Vision and Deep Learning
>  ? Overview of computer vision and deep learning
>  ? Basic Image Processing
>  ? History and development of deep learning in computer vision
>  ? Basic principles of artificial neural networks
>  2. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) - Review
>  3. Object Detection and Localization
>  ? Faster R-CNN, YOLO, SSD
>  4. Image Segmentation
>  ? Understanding image segmentation
>  ? Semantic segmentation with Fully Convolutional Networks (FCN)
>  ? Instance segmentation with Mask R-CNN, semantic segmentation with Encoder-Decoder Architecture, U-Nets
>  5. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)
>  ? Understanding GANs and their architecture
>  ? Training GANs for image generation and manipulation
>  ? Applications of GANs in computer vision - Super-Resolution
>  6. Diffusion-Based Generative Models - Applications- Text to Image- Captioning
>  7. Advanced Topics
>  ? Motion Estimation - Optical Flow
>  ? Structure From Motion
>  ? Depth Estimation
>  ? 3D Reconstruction
>  ? Rendering - Neural Radiance Fields
>  ? Medical image analysis with deep learning - Image Registration, Image Segmentation

**Official textbooks:** 1. Szeliski, Richard. Computer vision: algorithms and applications. Springer Nature, 2022.

**Official references:** Reference books:  1. Forsyth, D.A. and Ponce, J., 2002. Computer vision: a modern approach. prentice hall  2. Hartley, Richard, and Andrew Zisserman. Multiple view geometry in computer vision. Cambridge university  press, 2003

### From the PDF: DA3452

**Official description:** : This course introduces the computational methods used to process human language and speech. The course  covers the representation and processing of text and speech signals, statistical and neural language modeling,  and modern architectures such as transformers and large language models. Students will learn how machines  process language and speech through techniques including tokenization, probabilistic language models, neural  networks, and deep learning based models for speech recognition and synthesis.

**Official syllabus (trimmed):**

> Introduction to Speech and Language Technologies: Overview of speech and language technologies and their
>  applications. Historical development of the field and interdisciplinary foundations. Applications such as search
>  engines, conversational agents, translation systems, and voice assistants.
>  Words and Tokens: Representation of text in computational systems. Tokenization and normalization. Subword
>  tokenization methods. Edit distance algorithms and their applications.
>  N-gram Language Models: Probabilistic language modeling and the chain rule of probability. Unigram, bigram, and
>  higher order n gram models. Training and evaluation using perplexity.
>  Word Embeddings: Distributed representations of words and semantic similarity. Learning embeddings from large
>  corpora. Word2Vec models including CBOW and Skip Gram. GloVe embeddings and applications of word vectors
>  in downstream tasks.
>  Neural Networks for Language Processing: Neural language models. Representation learning for language.
>  Comparison with traditional language modeling approaches.
>  Transformers: Self attention mechanisms for sequence modeling. Encoder and decoder architectures. Multi head
>  attention and positional encodings. Transformers as the foundation of modern language models.
>  Large Language Models: Pretraining large neural language models using large scale corpora. Generative
>  language modeling objectives. 

**Official textbooks:** 1. Daniel Jurafsky and James H. Martin. Speech and Language Processing. 3rd Edition. Stanford University

**Official references:** 1. Lewis Tunstall, Leandro von Werra, Thomas Wolf. Natural Language Processing with Transformers. O’Reilly  Media. 2022.
