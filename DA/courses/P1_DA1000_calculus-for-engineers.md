## Roadmap

1. Sequences, limits, series, Taylor and Fourier series.
2. Continuity, Lipschitz, derivatives, gradients, Jacobian, Hessian.
3. Newton's method and gradient descent as applications.

## What your IITM course sheet says

### From the PDF: DA1000

**Official description:** The goal of this course is to equip engineers with the mathematical tools that they would primarily require for data  science and AI. The course will emphasize on understanding and applications of the concepts. On successful  completion of the course, participants should be able to:  - understand limits, convergence, gradients, and continuity.  - solve differential and integral equations.  - apply these concepts to analyze iterative algorithms, geometric problems, and optimization.  - create models of systems and data using calculus, and analyze their properties.

**Official syllabus (trimmed):**

> 1. Sequences, limits, convergence theorems and tests.
>  a. Applications to iterative algorithms.
>  2. Series, power series, Taylor series and Fourier series.
>  a. Applications to approximation models.
>  3. Continuity, smoothness, Lipschitz, universal approximation theorems, JL lemma.
>  a. Applications to quantifying neural network robustness.
>  4. Derivatives, subdifferentials, gradients, Jacobian, Hessian.
>  a. Applications to solve optimization problems.
>  5. Root-finding algorithms, Newton’s method, gradient descent and acceleration techniques.
>  a. Applications to train neural networks.
>  6. Evaluating gradients and integrals.

**Official textbooks:** 1. E. Kreyszig, Advanced Engineering Mathematics, 10th Ed., John Willey & Sons.  2. G. Strang, Calculus, Cambridge press, 3rd ed.  3. A. Beck, First-Order Methods in Optimization, SIAM, 2017.  4. Y. Nesterov, Lectures on Convex Optimization, Springer, 2018.
